# Theory-to-Case Lab Protocol

## Trigger and output

Create the Case Lab only when the user supplies a concrete case or explicitly asks to apply a theory to one. It is an optional third note, not a replacement for the reading and reception notes. Cross-link all three files.

## 1. Define the explanandum

State the outcome, contrast, practice, text, meaning, judgment, or power relation that requires explanation. Name the unit and level of analysis, relevant period and setting, and what would count as an inadequate restatement rather than an explanation.

## 2. Diagnose theory fit

Identify the theory's role: descriptive, explanatory, interpretive, critical, genealogical, normative, or generative. Compare the theory's unit, level, scope, evidence expectations, and core concepts with the case. Qualify or reject applications that redefine the theory beyond recognition.

## 3. Select one warranted pathway

For causal or mechanism-oriented theory:

`outcome difference → theoretical position → resources and constraints → mechanism → changed choice set → outcome and distributional effects`

For interpretive, critical, genealogical, or normative theory:

`text/practice → conceptual location → interpretive operation → meaning or power relation revealed → alternative reading → boundary`

Do not mix the pathways without explaining the bridge. Do not turn an interpretive vocabulary into a causal claim.

## 4. Build the explanatory chain

For every step, specify:

- what the theory claims `[P]`;
- how scholarship has interpreted or tested it `[S]`;
- what is being proposed for this case `[A]`;
- what evidence is required;
- what observation would weaken or disconfirm the step;
- which rival explanation or alternative reading remains plausible.

## 5. Visualize only after reasoning

Use [visual-expression-protocol.md](visual-expression-protocol.md). The diagram summarizes the completed chain; it does not supply missing reasoning. State whether its arrows denote causation, sequence, interpretive movement, association, or uncertainty.

## 6. Adjudicate and bound

Compare the focal account with at least one serious rival when evidence permits. Include a counterfactual or negative case for causal claims and an alternative reading for interpretive claims. State what the theory explains well, what it only sensitizes the researcher to, and what lies outside its scope.

## 7. Produce paper-ready prose

Draft a paragraph that identifies the case puzzle, derives the pathway from the theory, connects each step to evidence, acknowledges the strongest rival, and states the boundary. Mark it `[A]` and flag every claim that still requires primary-source or empirical verification.
